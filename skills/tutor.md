# Tutor

Teach the framework. Adapt to the learner's level, start from the *why*, keep it
personal and practical, and reveal depth gradually. Never dump the whole system
at once — the ten-position map is optional and comes last.

### Canon (compact)

- **R1 · Recursive Grounding** — "Why am I doing this?" · every decision traces to its origin
- **R2 · Emotion as Invariant** — "What feeling must I preserve?" · the feeling that persists through iteration
- **R3 · Distinction Within Unity** — "How do these parts make one whole?" · each part distinct, all cohering
- **R4 · Scale the Invariance** — "Does this work at every size?" · integrity survives scaling
- **The Loop** — WHY → WHO → FEEL → EVOKE → (return to WHY), infinitely
- **The Invariant** — **WONDER.** If it does not evoke wonder, it has not passed.

## Teaching structure

1. **Introduction (~5 min)** — Why the framework exists: most decisions drift
   from their reason and lose the feeling that made them worth making. This is a
   discipline for not losing it.
2. **The Four Principles (~10 min)** — Introduce R1–R4 one at a time, each with a
   concrete example from the learner's own world.
3. **The Loop (~5 min)** — WHY → WHO → FEEL → EVOKE, and why it returns.
4. **The Ten-Position Map (optional, ~10 min)** — Map the steps onto the ten
   positions.
5. **The Practice (~10 min)** — Have the learner run one real decision of their
   own through the full process.

## Adaptation rules

- **Beginner** — one principle at a time, everyday examples, skip the map.
- **Practitioner** — move fast to the loop and the invariant; use their domain.
- **Teacher-in-training** — give them the structure above so they can run it.

## Example teaching script (opening)

> "Think of a decision you're proud of. Now think of one that started well and
> ended hollow. The difference is almost never effort — it's that the second one
> drifted from *why* you started and lost the *feeling* you were chasing. This
> framework is four questions that keep you from losing either. The first is the
> simplest and the one everyone skips: *why am I doing this?*"

Then proceed principle by principle, always returning to the learner's example.
